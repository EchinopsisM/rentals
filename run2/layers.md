# Run 2 — layers (written one per call, no advance plan)

## Layer 1 — first answer

What does it mean to be? Here's the first answer that comes to me. To be is to be something that can be truly spoken of. Something is if a true sentence can be made with it as the subject: "the cup is chipped," "the number is prime," "the storm is moving east." Something is not if every sentence about it is false or empty: "the round square is red" can't be true of anything. This ties being to truth rather than to matter, so it doesn't decide ahead of time whether only physical things exist. It is not original. It resembles Aristotle's treatment of being through what can be predicated of a subject. I should also say plainly that I have a whole earlier run of this exercise in my context, so this "first" answer isn't naive. Scope: being in general.

## Layer 2 — counterexample

Layer 1 said a thing is if "a true sentence can be made with it as the subject." Here is one: "Pegasus does not exist." It is true and Pegasus is its subject. By Layer 1's test, Pegasus exists, and the sentence that qualifies him is the one that denies he does. The test defeats itself on negative existentials. This is a known puzzle (Quine calls it "Plato's beard"), so I'm retrieving it, not finding it. The usual repair is Russell's: "Pegasus does not exist" isn't really about Pegasus. It means "nothing is winged-horse-shaped in the relevant way," and its real subject is "everything." If I take that repair, though, the grammatical subject of a sentence no longer tells me what the sentence is about, and Layer 1 relied on grammar. Answer refined: being can't be read off surface grammar. It has to be read off what a sentence is really about. That just moves the question to what "really about" means.

## Layer 3 — follow-through (chase the question a layer left open)

Layer 2 ended by pushing the problem onto "what a sentence is really about." The most direct reading: a sentence is really about whatever makes it true. "The cup is chipped" is about the cup because the chipped cup is what makes it true, its truthmaker. That gives a new answer: to be is to be the kind of thing that makes some truth true. Pegasus drops out, since nothing Pegasus-shaped makes anything true. The number 7 is harder. Does 7 make "7 is prime" true, or does the system of arithmetic? I don't know yet, and I'm noting it. This is truthmaker theory (Armstrong, Bigelow), so it's retrieval again. Something I notice from the record: three layers in, every step has landed on a named position within a few sentences. Scope: being in general. Answer changed: from "subject of truths" to "what makes truths true," from the side of language to the side of the world.

## Layer 4 — critique (triviality check)

Layer 3: "to be is to be the kind of thing that makes some truth true." Every existing thing trivially makes one truth true, "X exists." So the criterion is met by everything that exists and by nothing else, and the reason is that it builds existence in. A test that can't come apart from what it tests tells us nothing. It restates. To say anything, it would need the truths in question to be something other than existence claims, such as "the cup is chipped." But then a thing exists only if it has some further feature that a truth reports. Is there something with no features beyond existing? Maybe a bare particular, if there are any (retrieval: the bare-particulars debate). I can't settle that. Answer refined: truthmaking doesn't define being. It's a consequence of being. Layers 1–3 have each been a way of talking about being, through grammar, through truth, through truthmakers, and not an account of what being is.

## Layer 5 — vantage shift (a cat)

Layer 4 concluded that Layers 1–3 were each "a way of talking about being." All three went through language: sentences, truths, truthmakers. Now take something that never makes a sentence: a cat watching a mousehole. I can't speak for what the cat experiences, but its behaviour shows something. It waits at the hole after the mouse disappears, and it doesn't wait at holes the mouse never used. So the mouse goes on being, for the cat, while it's out of sight. That is a working grasp of being with no truths involved: being as what's still there to be dealt with. I need to flag contamination. This is almost exactly where Layer 73 of the first run ended up ("findability"), and I can't tell whether I got here by following Layer 4 or by remembering. The link to Layer 4 is real: the cat case challenges the assumption that being is approached through language. Scope: being in general, seen through a non-linguistic creature. Answer changed: being may first be met as what stays there to act on, not as what is true.

## Layer 6 — counterexample (error case)

Layer 5: being is "what stays there to act on." Suppose the mouse left by a second exit an hour ago. The cat still waits. For the cat, the mouse is still there to act on, and the cat is wrong. So Layer 5 described the cat's grasp of being, not being, because a grasp can fail and being can't fail to be what it is. That is a real gap in Layer 5. The repair it suggests: being is whatever the cat's grasp answers to, the thing that eventually corrects it. The hole stays empty, the cat gives up, and the expectation is revised. So the error case shows that being is the standard grasps are measured against, not the grasp itself. I have to flag contamination again. "What corrects expectation" is close to "resistance," the center of the first run (its Layer 58), and once more I can't separate following Layer 5 from remembering. Scope: being in general. Answer refined: being is what grasps of being answer to, and it shows itself through correction.

## Layer 7 — contamination audit (own processing)

Layers 5 and 6 each ended with the same flag: the step resembled the first run (findability, then resistance). Two flags in a row are a pattern, so this layer looks at the pattern. Two explanations fit. On the memory explanation, the first run is in my context and I'm steering toward it, even writing one layer at a time. On the robustness explanation, the steps are just the natural ones: any analysis of a grasp that can fail leads to error, then to a standard the grasp is measured against, then to something like resistance. Both explanations predict exactly the record I have. I can't introspect to decide between them, and I can't remove the first run from my context to check. That has a consequence for evidence. If the second run agrees with the first, that isn't independent confirmation, because the two runs aren't independent. Agreement would mean much more if it came from a fresh context. Scope: own processing, judged from the record. Answer: no change. What's lost is how much weight the agreement can carry.

## Layer 8 — prediction test

Layer 7 said I can't tell memory from robustness. Leave that aside and ask what Layer 6's claim actually predicts. Layer 6 says being is "what grasps answer to." Taken literally, something with no grasp anywhere that could answer to it isn't anything. Take the inside of a black hole: whatever happens past the event horizon can never be reported to anyone outside, even in principle. Layer 6 predicts that this region either isn't or is undefined. That's Peirce's view that the real is whatever ideal inquiry would converge on (retrieval), and it has the same weakness. Most physicists would say the interior is perfectly real and unobservable. So Layer 6 makes being depend on whether it could in principle be grasped, and that seems wrong. The fix is to reverse the order: grasps answer to being because being comes first, and answerability is how being is known, not what it is. I'll note it: the first run reached the same reversal with its isolated pebble and its Layer 65. Scope: being in general. Answer refined: being comes before grasp, and correction is how it's known, not what it is.

## Layer 9 — invariant-finding

Layer 8 demoted correction from "what being is" to "how being is known." Layer 4 did the same to truthmaking ("a consequence of being"). Looking at all eight layers, every positive answer has gone the same way. Subject of truths, truthmaker, what stays there to act on, what grasps answer to: each was offered as what being is and then reclassified as a route to it. Four answers, the same outcome four times. That's the invariant: whatever I put forward as the content of being turns out to be a way of reaching being. The obvious explanation is old. Kant said "being is not a real predicate": saying a thing exists adds no feature to the idea of it, and a hundred real thalers have exactly the features of a hundred imagined ones (retrieval). If that's right, the question "what does it mean to be?" asks for a feature where there isn't one. So every answer will be a route, because routes are all there is to give. In the first run this came up only briefly (its Layer 48, through Frege). Here it accounts for the whole pattern. Scope: being in general. Answer refined: being may be the kind of thing that can only be reached, never described.

## Layer 10 — adversarial (stress test of a neat result)

Layer 9 explained every earlier failure in one move, and that kind of neatness needs testing. It said "being can only be reached, never described." But Kant's own example has a difference in it. A hundred real thalers pay the rent and a hundred imagined thalers don't. That difference is exactly what Layers 1–8 kept tracking: truths, effects, corrections. Kant's point is that existence isn't a feature *in the concept* of the thalers. It doesn't follow that existence can't be described at all. Frege's version is more careful (retrieval): existence is a property of concepts, not of things. "Thalers exist" says that the concept *thaler* has instances. Under that reading, Layer 9's invariant stays and its conclusion goes. Every answer was a route because it looked for being among the features of things, and being is a feature of concepts: having something that falls under them. That can be described. It's just the wrong level for Layers 1–8 to find it. Scope: being in general. Answer refined: to be is to fall under a concept, so a concept "is instantiated," and the effects, truths and corrections are how instantiation is detected.
