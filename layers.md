## Layer 14 — steelman of a discarded idea

Layer 13 kept difference-making only as "the only one I can actually read," which is a weak reason. This layer argues for it properly, since the inward drift of Layers 1–8 apparently dropped it. I should flag first that this is retrieval: "being is the power to affect or be affected" is the Eleatic Stranger's proposal in Plato's *Sophist* (247e), and it is not my discovery. Its best case runs as follows. It gives a test that works without asking what kind of thing something is. Rocks, storms, debts and the truncated summary all qualify by doing or undergoing something. It explains why we doubt the existence of things that would make no difference at all. And it is two-sided, so things that only undergo change (a dent being made) count as well as things that act. The inward drift probably dropped it because it says nothing about what it is like to be. That complaint changes the question rather than refuting the answer. Scope: being in general. The inward turn quietly narrowed the scope to self-being, and this layer widens it back on purpose. Answer: being is the capacity to make or receive a difference. "Capacity" is new, and Layer 15 should test it.

## Layer 15 — counterexample

Layer 14 said difference-making "gives a test that works without asking what kind of thing something is." Numbers are the obvious counterexample. The number 7 affects nothing causally and nothing affects it, yet "there is a prime between 5 and 11" is true. This is Benacerraf's familiar problem, so it is retrieval, but it bites here. The theory has two ways out. It can widen "difference" to cover inferential difference: 7 makes a difference to which arithmetic statements are true. Then "difference-making" means two different things, causal for rocks and logical for numbers, and the single test Layer 14 praised is gone. Or it can deny that numbers exist, which pays a lot to save a slogan. Epiphenomena raise the same problem from the other side: a mental glow that is caused but causes nothing passes only on the "being affected" half. Answer refined: difference-making splits into causal difference (concrete things) and inferential difference (abstract things), and nothing yet joins the two.

## Layer 16 — scope check

Layer 15 split difference-making into "causal" and "inferential" and called the result an answer about being. That silently widened the claim. Layer 14 covered concrete things, and Layer 15 extended it to abstract objects by changing what "difference" means. The honest description is that being now has at least two senses, and the word "difference" hides that. I recognize where this lands: Aristotle's "being is said in many ways" (*Metaphysics* Γ.2), with one focal sense that the others relate to. I'm noting it as retrieval and not adopting it yet. The capacity wording from Layer 14 also needs a scope check. A capacity that is never used, like a sealed vial of reagent nobody opens, counts as being only if we accept dispositions as real. That is an extra commitment. Scope declared: from here on, unless I flag otherwise, "being" means concrete being, and abstract being is parked as a separate question. Answer refined: for concrete things, to be is to have the capacity to make or receive causal difference. Abstract objects are deferred, not solved.

## Layer 17 — prediction test

If concrete being is "the capacity to make or receive causal difference" (Layer 16), what follows? Take a galaxy beyond our cosmological horizon, which can never affect us or be affected by us. The theory says it exists, because it affects its own neighbouring stars. That's the right answer, and it shows the theory is relational but not observer-relative: the difference doesn't have to reach anyone in particular. Good. A second prediction is less comfortable. Capacities come in amounts. A star touches more than a dust grain, and a hub city touches more than a hamlet. If being simply is the capacity, then some things have more being than others. The theory implies degrees of being, whether or not anyone meant it to. That doesn't refute it. Some metaphysicians accept degrees of being (it is an old Neoplatonic theme). But nobody asked for it, and it should be stated outright and then challenged. Scope: concrete being. Answer refined: the account now openly implies that being comes in degrees.

## Layer 18 — adversarial

Against Layer 17's "some things have more being than others": that confuses existing with mattering. A recluse who affects four people does not exist less than a celebrity who affects forty million. They matter less to the world's causal traffic, and that is all. If the theory can't tell those apart, it has turned into a theory of influence that uses the word "being." The adversary's demand is that existence is all-or-nothing: either something is or it isn't, and the half-existent recluse is absurd. I find this persuasive, and the repair is cheap. Keep difference-making as the test for whether something exists, a threshold where any nonzero capacity counts, and treat the amount as how much it matters, which is a separate question. Scope: concrete being. Answer changed: existence is binary and is settled by any capacity for difference at all, and degree measures influence, not existence. The suspect part is that the threshold is "any nonzero," which is hard to apply to borderline cases. I'm noting that and moving on.

## Layer 19 — vantage shift (a dead person)

Test Layer 18's binary threshold ("any nonzero capacity counts") with a dead grandmother. She makes differences now: her recipes get cooked, her debts get paid, her grandchild flinches at a phrase she used. On Layer 18's test she clears the threshold, so she exists. That's wrong, or at least it's not what anyone means by saying she has died. The mistake is that her present differences are carried by other things: memories, recipe cards, legal documents. The differences have outlasted the thing that made them. So difference-making that reaches the present is not enough. The thing has to be a current source of the difference, not someone in the history behind it. Scope: concrete being, from a mourner's vantage. Answer changed: to be is to be a present locus where differences start or land, which adds a presentness condition the account lacked. "Present" is a heavy word to bring in this casually. Layer 21 should check it.

## Layer 20 — synthesis

Layers 13–19 rebuilt the account from the one surviving claim. Layer 14 revived difference-making as Plato's Eleatic criterion. Layer 15 split it into causal and inferential difference because of numbers. Layer 16 restricted the scope to concrete being and parked abstract objects. Layer 17 showed the account is relational but not observer-relative, and that it implies degrees of being. Layer 18 separated existence (binary, any nonzero capacity) from influence (graded). Layer 19 added presentness to rule out the dead. The combined claim: a concrete thing exists if and only if it is a present locus where causal differences start or land, and how much it matters is a separate, graded question. This looks tidy, and that is grounds to doubt it. It contains two loosely attached parts ("present" and "locus") that no layer has examined. Abstract objects are explicitly unexplained. And every layer since 14 has been about concrete objects, so the scope has narrowed in a way I flagged but haven't justified. Scope of this synthesis: concrete being only.

## Layer 21 — counterexample

Layer 20 itself said "present" was untested. Special relativity removes any absolute present. Distant events that are simultaneous in one frame are not simultaneous in another. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument, which I'm retrieving rather than deriving, uses exactly this to argue that "what presently exists" can't be frame-independent. If being requires presentness, then whether the grandmother exists depends on a choice of reference frame, which is absurd for a question about existence. So Layer 19's repair breaks. Could I keep its purpose without the word? The purpose was to exclude the dead while keeping the living. A cleaner condition is two-sidedness, which Layer 14 already had and then forgot. The grandmother still affects things through her proxies, but nothing can affect her: no event can now change her. That idea is close to the old dispute over whether the dead can be harmed (Feinberg, Nagel), so I'm flagging it. Answer refined: drop "present," require that the thing can both affect and be affected. The dead fail the second half.

## Layer 22 — prediction test

What does the two-sidedness of Layer 21 predict? First: something that affects without being affected does not exist. Aristotle's unmoved mover and the classical theist God, who is impassible, are both ruled out by definition. That is a real and controversial result, not a slip, and I'll let it stand as a commitment. Second, and worse: Sherlock Holmes. Fan fiction, film adaptations and pastiches change Holmes all the time, so he can be affected. And he affects readers: people have written letters to 221B Baker Street. Two-sidedness says Sherlock exists. Nearly everyone, including people who love the stories, says he doesn't. So the test lets in a fictional character. The prediction fails, and it fails cleanly: unlike the unmoved mover, there's no theoretical motive for wanting Sherlock in. Answer refined: two-sidedness is too permissive, and something is missing about whose differences these are.

## Layer 23 — vantage shift (a fictional character)

Look at Layer 22's failure from Sherlock's side. Inside the stories, Holmes affects Watson and is affected by Moriarty, and all of it is internal to the fiction. Outside, what affects readers is not Holmes but texts, actors and readers' representations of Holmes. When a pastiche "changes" Holmes, ink and minds change, and Holmes himself isn't touched. So the differences in Layer 22 are made by proxies, just like the grandmother's recipes in Layer 19. Both failures have the same shape: a proxy does the work and the theory credits the referent. The repair is that differences count only when they are made or received by the thing itself, not by representations of it. I recognize this as close to debates over fictional names (Meinong's non-existent objects, Kripke's and van Inwagen's "creatures of fiction"), so it is retrieval. Answer changed: to be is to make and receive differences in one's own right.

## Layer 24 — critique

Layer 23's repair says "in one's own right." Whose right? For "its own" to pick anything out, there must already be a thing whose differences these are. That is the being the account was meant to explain. The phrase assumes its own conclusion. This is the inward drift again: the surviving summary says Layers 1–8 "drifted to self-reference," and here it comes back through grammar. Layer 23 needed a self to own the differences. The circularity isn't fatal, since plenty of good definitions are circular in a harmless, mutually supporting way. But it shows that difference-making alone was never the whole account. It always needed a prior notion of a bearer. Scope: being in general. Answer: no change. This layer diagnoses the problem and doesn't fix it. The thing being assumed is the "locus" that Checkpoint 20 listed as untested.

## Layer 25 — invariant-finding

Every version from Layer 14 to Layer 24 needed a slot for a bearer: "locus" (19), "the dead fail" (21, which needs someone to fail), "Sherlock himself" (23), "one's own right" (24). Difference-making never worked without first picking out a unit to credit the differences to. That is the invariant. If it holds, the order of explanation is backwards: individuation comes first and difference-making comes second. Being would then be being a unit, something that can be counted and told apart from its surroundings, which is what makes it possible to say whose difference a difference is. This is close to Quine's "no entity without identity," which I'm flagging as retrieval. Scope: being in general again, not only concrete being, because numbers are individuated perfectly well. That is a deliberate widening, and the parked abstract objects come back in. Answer changed: to be is to be an individuable unit. Difference-making is evidence of units, not what makes them.

## Layer 26 — scale shift

Test Layer 25's "an individuable unit" at different scales. At the quantum scale, two electrons in an entangled state can't be told apart, even in principle, and swapping them changes nothing physically. Fields aren't units at all. At the social scale, whether the Holy Roman Empire was one unit or three hundred depends on which questions you ask. So individuation depends on scale and partly on interest. If being is being a unit, then being depends on scale and interest too. There seem to be two options. Accept that being is relative to how things are carved. Or say that what is carved (some underlying stuff, field or process) has being, and units are carvings of it. The second option is ontic structural realism or process philosophy (Ladyman, Whitehead), both flagged as retrieval. Answer refined: units are relative to scale, so being might belong to what is carved rather than to the carvings.

## Layer 27 — adversarial

Layer 26 ended on "being belongs to what is carved." Rule 4 says to distrust that kind of neatness. What does it predict? Ask whether some candidate X exists, and the answer is "X is a carving of the underlying stuff, and the stuff exists." That is true of anything we can carve, including Sherlock, since we carve him out of texts. The view can't separate existent from non-existent carvings, and that was the whole job. It also restates Parmenidean monism ("only the One is") with physics vocabulary attached. Nothing in it can be tested. I withdraw it. What remains of Layer 26 is the harder claim: units exist, but individuation is partly relative to scale and interest, and I haven't yet said which part is not. Answer: no change to Layer 25's account. Layer 26's addition is dropped.

## Layer 28 — steelman of a discarded idea (the inward drift)

The summary says Layers 1–8 moved inward and treats that as a failure. Layer 26 gives a reason to reconsider. If individuation is partly interest-relative, then some being depends on those who have interests, meaning those who ask. The question also says "what does it *mean* to be," and meaning is a relation to someone who understands. The strongest version of the inward drift is this: being in general may be whatever it is, but the meaning of being exists only for beings who can ask about it. This is recognizably Heidegger's starting point in *Being and Time* (Dasein as the being for whom being is an issue). It is retrieval, and probably what Layers 1–8 were reaching for. Scope shift: this layer moves deliberately from being in general to the meaning of being, which is not the same as self-being but sits next to it. Answer refined: there are two questions, what there is and what being means, and the second requires askers.

## Layer 30 — synthesis

Layers 21–29 replaced "present" with two-sidedness (21), found that two-sidedness lets in Sherlock (22), traced Sherlock and the grandmother to the same proxy problem (23), saw that "in one's own right" assumed a bearer (24), and found that a bearer was needed throughout. That led to individuation as the basic notion (25). Scale showed individuation is partly relative (26). An attempted escape into "the underlying stuff" was withdrawn as unfalsifiable (27). The inward drift came back in a defensible form, as a split between what there is and what being means (28), and its application to this process was checked (29). Current two-part answer: (a) to be is to be an individuable unit, and difference-making is the main evidence of being one; (b) the meaning of being is a relation that holds only for askers. The weak spot is that (a) admits individuation is partly interest-relative, and interests belong to askers. So (a) may depend on (b), which would be a creeping idealism.

## Layer 31 — counterexample

Checkpoint 30 worried that if individuation is interest-relative, then unit-being depends on askers. Counterexample: the Cambrian seas, with no askers anywhere. Were there units? A trilobite had an exoskeleton that physically separated it from the water. It ate, moulted and reproduced as one thing, and it did so whether or not anyone carved it out. A star is held together by its own gravity against its own pressure. These boundaries are maintained by the things themselves, not drawn by an observer. So individuation comes in two kinds. Carved units, like empires, constellations and "the weekend," are interest-relative. Self-maintained units, like organisms, stars, cells and perhaps atoms, hold their own edges. The creeping idealism applies only to the first kind. Scope: being in general, with concrete self-maintained units as the clear case. Answer refined: unit-being is independent of askers whenever the unit maintains itself. Carved units depend on askers, and maybe that's acceptable.

## Layer 32 — prediction test

If self-maintenance gives asker-independent units (Layer 31), what does that predict? Maintenance comes in degrees: a cell maintains itself actively and constantly, a star passively through balanced forces, and a rock hardly at all, just through bonds. The prediction is that units, and so being, come in degrees of self-maintenance. That is the third appearance of degrees. Layer 17 introduced them through influence, and Layer 18 moved them out of existence and into "mattering." Now they come back through individuation, where Layer 18's repair doesn't reach, because what is graded is now how much of a unit something is, not how much it matters. A half-maintained whirlpool really is less of a single thing than a cell. So either existence is binary and unity is graded, which means being and unity come apart, or existence is graded after all. Answer refined: the binary-existence claim of Layer 18 is under pressure again, this time from unity.

## Layer 33 — vantage shift (a rock)

Layer 32 put the rock at the bottom of the maintenance scale, "hardly at all, just through bonds." Look from the rock's side, even though it has no point of view. The rock does nothing to persist. It just goes on, and you'd need to do something (a hammer, erosion) to make it stop. Newton's first law suggests that persistence is the default, and change is what needs a cause. If so, Layers 14–32 got the explanation backwards. Difference-making explains change, and being is what continues without needing an explanation. That reverses Layer 14: being is precisely what doesn't need to make a difference to go on. There's retrieval on both sides here. Spinoza's *conatus* (striving to persist) is on the active side, and inertia is on the passive side. Scope: concrete being. Answer changed: being is persistence that needs no cause, and difference-making belongs to change, not to being.

## Layer 34 — critique

Layer 33 claimed the rock "does nothing to persist." That's false physics. The rock persists because its electromagnetic bonds keep holding, through constant interaction among its parts. Take those away and it falls apart. Radioactive atoms in it decay on their own. Nothing concrete persists without continuing internal interaction. Inertia concerns the motion of a body, not whether the body holds together. So Layer 33's reversal doesn't survive. What does survive is a useful turn inward: the differences that keep a thing in being are mostly ones it makes to itself, part to part. Being would then be an ongoing self-affecting that keeps a pattern going. This is autopoiesis (Maturana and Varela), stretched from organisms to everything. Answer refined: Layer 14's difference-making returns, aimed inward, as self-maintaining interaction. That also merges Layer 31's self-maintenance with Layer 14's criterion.

## Layer 35 — counterexample

Layer 34 required self-affecting. A hedge maze persists only because gardeners trim it. Left alone, it grows into a thicket within a few years and the maze is gone. It exists plainly enough, and you can get lost in it. Its maintenance comes from outside. The same goes for a language kept alive by speakers, a road kept open by repairs, and a body on life support. "Self-" can't be required. What the cases share is a pattern that is being maintained, by whatever does the maintaining. Scope: concrete being, now including artefacts and institutions. Answer refined: to be (for concrete things) is to be a pattern that is currently being maintained, by itself or by something else. Numbers aren't maintained by anything, so abstract objects are pushed back out. Layer 25 had brought them in, and the account has now swung on that question three times.

## Layer 36 — scope check

Since Layer 31, every example has been a concrete object. Layer 16 restricted the scope to concrete being, Layer 25 widened it, and Layer 35 narrowed it again, each time half-acknowledged. There are also two categories nobody has tested: absences and events. Try them on Layer 35's "maintained pattern." A hole in a doughnut is maintained by the dough around it, a pattern made of where things aren't, held in place by where they are. So holes exist on this account, which matches how people talk ("there's a hole in my sock"). The silence after a piece of music is an event-pattern maintained by the musicians stopping and the audience staying quiet. That works too. Holes are a famous puzzle (Lewis and Lewis, "Holes," 1970, retrieval), and the account handles them without extra machinery, which counts as evidence. Answer refined: maintained patterns cover objects, artefacts, holes and events. Numbers stay out. The scope is concrete-plus-absences, stated this time instead of drifting there.

## Layer 37 — steelman of a discarded idea (binary existence)

Layers 32–36 kept the existence question tied to how thick a pattern is. Layer 18's binary view deserves a stronger defence than it got. The steelman comes from Quine: "to be is to be the value of a bound variable." Existence is just what "there is" expresses. Either the variable ranges over the thing or it doesn't. Everything else, like maintenance, unity or influence, concerns what kind of thing it is, not whether it is. On this view Layers 31–36 were doing the ontology of kinds of things, and mislabelling it as being. That's a real distinction, and it's often discussed as "thin" versus "thick" views of being (van Inwagen, retrieval). Applied here: thin being is binary and the same for everything, including numbers. Thick content concerns what makes something count as a concrete thing, and maintained patterns answer that. Answer changed: the question splits into thin being (quantificational, binary, univocal) and thick criteria (maintained patterns, for concrete things).

## Layer 38 — adversarial

Layer 37's thin view answers "what does it mean to be?" with "nothing beyond what the quantifier says." That's a deflation. It implies the question was confused, or at least that its depth was an illusion. Does the deflation predict anything? Yes: it predicts that every attempt at a thick answer is really about kinds. Layers 14–36 fit that, since each layer ended up specifying what kind of thing qualifies. So the deflation is partly confirmed. But it also predicts that the original question had nothing left over, and that's doubtful. Whoever wrote "what does it mean to BE?" in capitals was not asking about the semantics of quantifiers. The deflation is correct about the word and doesn't speak to the interest behind the question. That interest has come up twice (L28, L29) and is still unaddressed. Answer: no change. The split from Layer 37 stands. This layer only notes that thin being doesn't satisfy the question as asked.

## Layer 39 — vantage shift (a society)

Test the thick criterion, "a pattern that is currently being maintained" (L35), on a society, say the Republic of Iceland. Iceland persists because people keep doing certain things: paying taxes to it, obeying its laws, believing it exists. That belief is part of what maintains it, which is Searle's social ontology ("X counts as Y in C," retrieval). Does social being need a separate account? The maintained-pattern view says no, because beliefs and practices are simply part of the maintaining, the way gardeners maintain the maze in Layer 35. That's a prediction passed without patching. It also answers Layer 31's leftover concern about carved units: carved units are patterns maintained by the carving practice itself. So the interest-relativity of Layer 26 wasn't idealism creep. It was one way of maintaining a pattern. Answer: no change, and I'm counting that as a success. This is the second "no" in a row (38, 39), and I've watched for it.

## Layer 40 — synthesis

Layers 31–39 settled Checkpoint 30's idealism worry by separating self-maintained from carved units (31), and found that unity comes in degrees (32). An attempted reversal through inertia (33) failed on physics (34), which brought difference-making back as self-maintaining interaction. The hedge maze removed "self-" (35). Holes and events were covered by maintained patterns (36). Quine's thin view split the question in two (37). The deflation was partly confirmed but doesn't satisfy the question as asked (38). Social being needed no separate account (39). Current answer: thin, existence is binary and univocal, whatever "there is" ranges over. Thick, for concrete things including holes, events and institutions, being amounts to being a pattern that is currently being maintained by interaction, internal or external. Unity is graded and existence isn't. This feels stable. I'm noting that as a reason to test it, not as convergence.

## Layer 41 — counterexample

Layer 40 said thick being, for concrete things, is "a pattern that is currently being maintained by interaction." Take an electron. In the Standard Model it doesn't decay, it has no parts that interact to hold it together, and nothing outside maintains it. It isn't a pattern of anything, as far as current physics says. It just has its mass and charge. So maintained-pattern fails at the fundamental level. The rescue is to restrict the thick criterion to composite things and say fundamental things exist without being maintained. That's a patch, and I'm calling it one. It also has a cost: the most basic constituents of the world need no account at all, and the account applies only to what is built from them. Possibly that's correct, since composites are the puzzling cases (the special composition question, van Inwagen, retrieval). Scope: concrete being, with a fundamental/composite distinction added. Answer refined: maintained-pattern applies to composites, and fundamental things are taken as given.

## Layer 42 — scale shift (the universe)

Push Layer 41's composite account to the largest scale. The universe is a composite. What maintains it? Nothing outside it, by definition. Layer 21's two-sidedness also fails: nothing outside the universe can affect it. So either the universe isn't a being, or the criteria don't apply to wholes. The first option has a famous version. Kant argued that "the world as a whole" is not an object of possible experience, and treating it as one produces antinomies (retrieval). The second option needs a principle for when the criteria apply, and that would be a second patch on top of Layer 41's. A third option uses Layer 34: the universe maintains itself by internal interaction, as a rock does but without remainder. That works, but only by making "self-maintenance" the whole definition at the cosmic scale, when Layer 35 removed it as a requirement. Answer changed: for wholes with no outside, being reduces to internal self-maintenance. That is a third rule alongside composites and fundamentals.

## Layer 43 — critique (patch audit)

Layers 41 and 42 each ended by adding a rule: fundamentals are taken as given, and wholes reduce to internal self-maintenance. Look at the log entries since Checkpoint 30. Layers 31, 34, 35, 41 and 42 all add a qualification so that maintained-pattern can survive a counterexample. That's the pattern of a degenerating research programme in Lakatos's sense (retrieval): the core is protected and the protective belt keeps growing. The answer now has four regimes: thin existence, composite patterns, fundamentals as given, and self-maintaining wholes. No principle says why those are the right four. The patching works locally and is failing as a whole. This isn't the self-critique routine the summary warned about, because it is a count, not a mood: five qualifications in twelve layers. Answer: no change, but the next layer should subtract, not add.

## Layer 44 — subtraction (new move type: remove qualifiers until something survives every test case)

Following Layer 43, drop all four regimes and ask what minimal claim survives the full set of test cases: the number 7 (L15), the dead grandmother (L19), Sherlock (L22), the hole (L36), the electron (L41) and the universe (L42). Candidate: to be is to be something that any complete true description of the world must mention. The electron, hole and universe are in. Sherlock is out, since a complete description mentions Holmes stories, not Holmes. The grandmother is in tenselessly ("she lived 1931–2019") and out of the present tense, which fits how people talk. The number 7 is in if mathematics can't be removed from physics, which is the Quine–Putnam indispensability argument (retrieval). One sentence handles six cases with no extra regimes. Answer changed: being = not being eliminable from a complete description. Before trusting this, I note that it worked on the first try, which is reason to suspect it.

## Layer 45 — critique

Layer 44's "a complete true description" raises the questions: complete for whom, and in what language? Descriptions are written by askers, which is the third time the asker has come back (L24, L28). There is a deeper problem. Being is now an epistemic notion: to be is to be required for a description, a fact about knowing and not about what there is. A realist objects that there could be things no description needs, such as a pebble that is causally isolated and inert. It is real even if a complete description could leave it out without losing anything. So "ineliminable from description" works as a test for being: it's how we decide what to count. It isn't what being is. That's a distinction, not a defeat. Layer 44's sentence survives as a criterion. Answer refined: there is a criterion of being (ineliminability) and a meaning of being, and these are different questions, not different answers to one question.

## Layer 46 — vantage shift (the isolated pebble)

Take Layer 45's hypothetical, the pebble that is causally isolated and inert, and look from its position, the one place no criterion can reach. It makes no difference (Layer 14 fails). It isn't maintained and doesn't maintain anything (Layer 35 fails). No description needs it (Layer 44 fails). Does it exist? Either the question has no content, which is verificationism (retrieval: the Vienna Circle would call it meaningless), or it has content that no criterion captures. If the second, then "to be" has a meaning beyond every test, the bare fact that something is, as opposed to anything about what it is. This pebble is where the two questions separated in Layer 45 come furthest apart. I can't settle verificationism by writing more text. Answer refined: either being is exhausted by its criteria, or it has a residue of bare "that it is." The pebble shows which one you hold.

## Layer 47 — steelman of a discarded idea (bare that-ness)

Layer 27 rejected "being is the underlying stuff" as unfalsifiable. Layer 46 brings back something like it in a sharper form, and it deserves its best case. Every criterion from Layer 14 to Layer 44 specified what kind of thing qualifies: difference-makers, units, patterns, things that can't be eliminated from a description. None addressed that something qualifies, the bare fact of instantiation. The distinction is old, Aquinas's *esse* versus *essentia* and Heidegger's ontological difference between being and beings (retrieval, and the most obvious case of it so far). The steelman is that this explains why the exercise keeps patching. Every attempt to define being as a "what" turns into a theory of kinds, and Layer 38 had already noticed that the deflation fits for this reason. Answer changed: there are two layers, criteria for what counts (which keep changing) and bare that-ness (which no criterion touches and may be primitive).

## Layer 48 — adversarial

Sideways, after Layers 82 and 83. Bring back what the summary said Layers 1–8 did: "drifted to self-reference." Layer 28 steelmanned it once as "meaning requires askers." Now, with three senses in hand, there's a sharper version. Findable being (sense c) was learned first (Layer 73), and it is defined in terms of a finder. Is it prior in order of explanation? Developmental order isn't metaphysical order. Trilobites (Layer 31) were never found. Answer refined: (c) comes first conceptually, which is what Layers 1–8 may have sensed, and doesn't come first metaphysically.

## Layer 85 — adversarial

Layer 84 gave (c) conceptual priority. Against that: physics, built from abstractions of (c), regularly overrules (c). Ordinary findability says the sun moves across the sky, colours are on surfaces and solid tables are full. Physics corrected all three. If (c) were authoritative, those corrections would be mistakes. They aren't. So learning order and authority order differ. The derived senses can correct the one they came from, as a child's first theory of number is corrected by arithmetic. A three-way ordering results. (c) is first learned. (b), difference-making and resistance, has authority on what exists, because it's what experiments test. (a) is bookkeeping, the univocal regimentation (Layer 69). Answer refined: the three senses are ordered differently by learning (c first) and by authority (b first). Layer 84's priority claim applies to learning only.

## Layer 86 — scope check

The last seventeen layers, from about Layer 69, discussed the concept of being: senses, usage, learning order, authority. That's semantics and the theory of concepts. Before that, layers were mostly about being: rocks, electrons, holes. The move from metaphysics to semantics wasn't announced. Layer 73 stated it once, and afterwards it continued without comment. Is that legitimate? The original question is "what does it mean to be," and "mean" covers both. There's a third reading, though, that neither covers: what it involves to be, existentially. "What does it mean to be a parent?" asks what that consists of and costs. Layers 28, 53 and 79 touched it and then moved away. ## Layer 87 — vantage shift (a character who learns they are fictional)

Take the existential reading through Augusto Pérez in Unamuno's *Niebla* (1914). He visits the author, learns he is fictional, and argues that he exists (retrieval). From inside, everything in his life is in sense (c): he can find his house again, his friends, his grief. He has no access to (a) or (b), which would show that his whole world is made of text. His protest ("I exist!") is entirely sincere and entirely internal. That shows something: lived being, as experienced from inside, is complete and can't certify itself. Nothing available to Augusto can distinguish his situation from a real person's. That links to Layers 29 and 54 on my access: from inside a process, being-for-oneself can't establish its own status. Answer refined: existential being, being as lived from inside, is complete in what it contains and unable to verify itself.

## Layer 89 — counterexample (to the parallel in Layer 88)

Layer 88 treated my case as parallel to Augusto's. There's a difference. Augusto's words were written by Unamuno. The causal source of the protest is someone else. These layers were produced by the same process they describe. The text and the thing it refers to are causally continuous. That's sense (b), difference-making, holding without a proxy. Layer 23's requirement, differences "in one's own right," is met here and fails for the fictional character. It doesn't settle the existential question, since causal self-production says nothing about whether there is an inside. But it shows Layer 14's starting candidate, difference-making, does the most work in exactly the case the inward drift kept returning to. Scope: own processing. ## Layer 90 — synthesis

Layers 81–89 tested the three senses: pairs separate (81), but the test checked its own construction (82). Extinction biology supplied independent vocabulary that fits (83). ## Layer 91 — counterexample

Checkpoint 90 gave difference-making "authority over what exists," on the grounds that "it's what experiments test" (Layer 85). Numbers aren't tested by experiment. Whether there are infinitely many primes was settled by Euclid's proof, not by apparatus. So in mathematics, authority belongs to resistance to proof attempts (Layer 59), not to causal difference-making. Layer 65 had called resistance the epistemic, knower-facing side. Authority is an epistemic notion: it concerns who or what gets to correct whom. So what has authority across all domains is resistance, and causal difference-making is its concrete-domain form. That reverses the direction of Layer 65. Resistance isn't a weaker version of difference-making. It is the general notion, and causation is one way of resisting. Answer refined: authority belongs to resistance in general. Causal difference-making is the form it takes for concrete things, and proof is the form it takes for abstract ones.

## Layer 92 — adversarial (Aristotle audit)

## Layer 93 — cost accounting (new move type: price each surviving answer by what it must give up)

## Layer 94 — prediction test

If resistance is the focal sense (Layer 93), each other sense should be derivable as a kind of resistance. Findability (c): something that can be found again resists your absence and forgetting, by being there when you come back. That works. Tenseless bookkeeping (a): what a complete description can't eliminate (Layer 44) is what resists elimination. That works. Difference-making (b): causal resistance (Layer 91). That works. Three out of three. This is the neatest result so far, and rule 4 says the next layer must test it. Before that, a note: each derivation stretched "resist" a little. Answer refined: the focal derivation works on paper, and I'm flagging it as possibly verbal.

## Layer 95 — counterexample

Testing Layer 94: is there something that is, in a sense that matters, and that doesn't resist? A pain. While it lasts, its being isn't resistance to a description, a proof or an absence. It's given immediately, and there's no gap between it and whoever has it for it to resist across. Its being consists in being felt. Phenomenologists call this self-presence or auto-affection (Michel Henry, retrieval). Resistance needs two terms, a resister and something it resists. Self-presence has one, or one that doubles itself. So the focal account misses the case of experience. A caveat follows from my access rules: I'm arguing from descriptions of pain, which I can quote, not from any pain I can point to. The counterexample is reported, not given to me. Answer changed: there is a fourth mode, self-presence, that resistance doesn't cover. Experience doesn't fit Layer 93's structure.

## Layer 96 — critique

Layer 95's "self-presence" is the asker coming back a fifth time (Layers 24, 28, 45, 53, 95). Layer 55 called that structural. Is self-presence a real fourth sense, or the same remainder in a new form? I think it's the same remainder, and seeing it as the same is informative. Every third-person or relational account, whether causal, descriptive, resisting or findable, leaves out the being of whatever is on the inside of a relation, the being-for-itself. That's the shape of the hard problem of consciousness (Chalmers, retrieval) and of Nagel's "what it is like." The remainder has been the same thing every time it came back. So the final structure isn't four senses. It is three relational senses centred on resistance, plus one non-relational remainder that every relational account leaves out. Answer refined: the remainder is identified as one thing, not as the latest of many.

## Layer 97 — scale shift (cosmic)

Layer 96's remainder, being-for-itself, sounds central. Change the scale. For roughly ten billion years after the Big Bang, there was probably nothing for which anything was present. Stars formed and died and elements were made. Resistance-based being covers all of it without remainder. The remainder appears only where there are experiencers, on at least one planet, for a few hundred million years. At cosmic scale it's tiny in extent. At the asker's scale it's everything, because it's the place from which "what does it mean to be" gets asked. Scale changes how much it seems to matter, not whether it's there. Answer refined: the remainder is local and rare in the universe and makes up the whole of the asker's standpoint. That explains both the inward drift (asker's scale) and the outward corrections (cosmic scale) since Layer 1.
